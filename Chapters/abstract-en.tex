%-- contributing to the research of immersive \gls{VR} navigation in restricted physical spaces using Non-Euclidean virtual environments.
This dissertation aims to improve navigation in \gls{VR} within small physical spaces, by employing portals as a mean to \gls{RDW}. 
To address current limitations of traditional portals used in \gls{VR} three portal variants were designed and evaluated: 
Movable Portals, Interactive Doors and Revolving Doors 

\gls{VR} applications enable exploration of large \glspl{VE}, but the \glsfirst{UX} is limited by the 
typically small physical spaces available to users (2.4m~x~2.2m).
To address this limitation, various \gls{VR} locomotion techniques have been proposed. Controller-based techniques (e.g. teleport) provide 
a simple solution to this problem, by allowing users to navigate virtual worlds while standing still, 
but reduce immersion compared to natural walking.
\gls{RDW} techniques using portals preserve natural locomotion within confined spaces, 
yet their placement poses challenges: near boundaries they reduce preview of destinations, while further away they risk appearing unnatural.
This trade-off creates a design tension between maximizing navigable space and preserving the naturalness of the environment.

To address these challenges,
four different \gls{VR} portal variants were designed, implemented and studied, with them being:
(1) Traditional Portals, a baseline condition representing the common portals found in \gls{VR} applications and research; 
(2) Movable Portals, portals initially set on a wall with a continuous preview that users can reposition; 
(3) Interactive Doors, door-shaped portals embedded in a wall that opens interactively as a real door;
(4) Revolving Doors, portals offering a continuous transition between rooms through a circular motion.

A user study (N=31) assessed these four portal designs, focusing on usability, spatial
understanding, immersion, and perceived naturalness. Results suggest that the alternative designs addressed the portal limitations 
successfully and helped convey a continuous sense of space, with each portal design revealing their strong suits.
